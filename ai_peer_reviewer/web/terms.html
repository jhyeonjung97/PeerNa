Terms and privacy

PeerNa · last changed 31 August 2026

PeerNa is run by Hyeonjung Jung (jiuy97@stanford.edu). It reads a manuscript and writes a referee report. This page says what happens to what you upload, and what the report is and is not.

What the report is

Every comment is written by a language model. Some will be wrong. Some will describe things the manuscript does not say, and some real problems will be missed — in testing against papers with deliberately introduced errors, the tool found about four in ten of them.

Check every comment against the manuscript before acting on one or sending it to anyone. A report is a draft to work from, not a review to submit.

What you may upload

If you are refereeing for a journal, the manuscript is not yours to share. Elsevier, ACS, Springer Nature and NIH among others restrict uploading manuscripts under review to AI tools, and some prohibit it outright. Check the policy of the journal that asked you before uploading anything.

By uploading, you confirm that you are permitted to.

Where a manuscript goes

It is sent to this service, and from here to whichever model provider is configured — currently OpenAI, or another provider reached through a router. The provider processes it and, under its own terms, may retain it for a period. This service does not control that.

Two smaller things also leave: reference strings from the bibliography go to Crossref to check that cited works exist and have not been retracted, and the literature pass may run web searches. That pass is instructed to search in its own words and never to put a sentence, title, measured value, sample name or author from the manuscript into a query. Turning off citation verification stops the searches entirely.

What is kept, and for how long

WhatKept
The uploaded fileDeleted when the review finishes
The reportUntil pushed out by the fifty most recent reviews
Working notes from an interrupted reviewTwo days, then deleted
Your email address and how many reviews you have runUntil you ask for it to go

A finished report is readable only by the account that ran it. Nobody else can list your reviews or open one, including by guessing its address.

The operator can read what is on the server's disk. There is no encryption that would prevent that, and claiming otherwise would be untrue.

Deleting your data

Write to jiuy97@stanford.edu and everything held for your account will be removed. There is no self-service button for it yet.

Signing in

Sign-in goes through Google, which tells this service your email address and nothing else. It is stored in a signed cookie in your browser and used to keep your reviews separate from everyone else's and to count how many you have run.

Cost

Three reviews are free per account. Nothing takes payment at present; when the free reviews are gone the page says so.

No warranty

The service is provided as it is, without any guarantee that it works, is available, or is correct. It may change or stop at any time. It is a research tool run by one person, not a product with a support contract.
